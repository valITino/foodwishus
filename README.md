# FoodwishUs

**U cook. I eat.** A private meal app for the two of you. One proposes and cooks, the other
ranks the wishes, eats, takes the proof photo and hands out the ratings. Both get a Cockpit
full of numbers about what was eaten and how it went down.

It works like any app on your phone, but there is nothing to buy and no store to visit.
Everything you add is kept on your phones and shared between them through a small private
cloud that only the two of you can get into.

The app lives here: **https://valitino.github.io/foodwishus/**

## Put it on your Android phone

1. Open the link above in **Chrome**.
2. Tap the three dots in the top right corner.
3. Tap **Install app** (on some phones it says **Add to Home screen**).

From then on FoodwishUs has its own icon and opens like every other app. Do this on both
phones. A tablet or a computer works the same way, in its browser. You find the same offer
in the app under **Settings, This device**.

**It opens without a connection.** Once the app has been opened one time, it starts in the
train tunnel too. What you do there is saved on the phone and sent later.

**A new version.** When the app has been improved, a small note says so, with a **Reload**
button. Nothing reloads by itself while you are in the middle of something.

## Notifications

Each phone decides for itself. Open **Settings, Notifications**, tap **Turn on
notifications** and then **Allow** when Chrome asks. You then hear about five things: a new
dish or menu was proposed, a meal was planned, "Served, proof needed!", a verdict was handed
in, and the cook answered. Every kind has its own switch.

If the page says notifications are not set up for this kitchen yet, the owner of the cloud
has one more step to do (see "For the owner of the cloud").

## Open the kitchen and invite your partner

Only one of you does the first part.

1. Open the app and tap **Start from scratch**.
2. Choose who you are at the table: **I wear the apron** (the Chef) or **I hold the fork**
   (the Taster). You can also give the kitchen a name.
3. Type your name, build your avatar and write a status line if you feel like it.
4. Choose a **PIN** with six digits and tap **Open the kitchen**.
5. The app now shows an invitation: a QR code, a link and a short code. Show the QR code to
   your partner, or send the link.

Your partner opens the link (or taps **I have a code** and types the code), goes through
the same steps and taps **Join the kitchen**. That is it: the table is set for two.

Just curious? **Look around first** opens a demo kitchen with three months of sample meals.
Nothing in there is shared or counts. You leave it with **Leave the demo**.

## Who is who

- **Chef and Taster.** The Chef proposes dishes and cooks them. The Taster says how much
  they want each dish, eats, photographs and rates. The role you chose is only your usual
  one: for any single meal you can pick who cooks.
- **Your name and your PIN are your key.** On another phone, a tablet or after a new
  install, tap **Back to the kitchen** and type both. Your profile, your settings and the
  whole kitchen come along. You can be logged in on several devices at once.
- **Five wrong PINs** pause that name for 15 minutes, then longer each time, up to a day.
  That keeps strangers from guessing.
- **The admin** is whoever opened the kitchen. Under **Settings, People** the admin can
  invite the partner, let the partner back in after a forgotten PIN (a one-time code, then a
  new PIN), pause the partner's seat (nothing is deleted) and log the partner out
  everywhere, for example after a lost phone. The partner cannot do any of this to the admin.
- **Settings** are behind your avatar on Today: your profile, language (English or German),
  light or candlelight, showing or hiding calories, your PIN and devices.

## The five tabs

**Today.** What is on the table today, and what comes next. The Chef taps **Served** when
the food is on the table. The Taster then takes the proof photo, taps a face for the quick
verdict, and can add the details: spoons for taste, looks and the rest, portions, a few
words. A verdict can be changed for 24 hours, then it is sealed. The Chef can answer it.

**Wishlist.** Every dish and menu that was proposed. The Taster gives hearts for how much
they want it and drags the cards into order: the top one is the biggest wish. Tap a card to
pick a day for it, to skip it or to take it off the list.

**Week.** Our week as a calendar: what is planned for which day and meal, and what was
eaten. Tap a meal of a day (breakfast, lunch, dinner, dessert) to put something there:
from the wishlist, or anything from the recipe book. A meal that is taken still takes a
second dish, and the same dish can be planned as often as you like. Tap a planned meal to
open it.

**Any day is fine.** Wherever a day is picked, the next two weeks are there to tap and a
date field takes every day after them. On a dish, **Propose** asks where it goes: **On the
wishlist**, with as many good days as you like, or **On a day**, straight onto the calendar.

**Cockpit.** The numbers: your favourites, ratings over time, how well cravings predicted
the verdict, a calendar of hearts, the Hall of Fame and a few fun facts. The filter at the
top narrows everything down to a time, a dish, a course or a cook. Tap a dish to see its
own page of numbers and photos.

**Recipes.** The recipe book with all your dishes and menus. A dish has ingredients, steps,
times and, if you like, calories. **Cook mode** shows one step at a time in big letters,
with timers. When the last step is done, **Served** is right there. The round **+**
button in the middle of the bar adds a new dish, a recipe from a link, something for the
basket, a menu, or a meal for today.

All proof photos hang together on the **photo wall**, which you reach from Today or from
any meal that has been eaten.

The **bell** on Today (under your picture) collects what the other one did: a new wish, a
plan, "served", a verdict, an answer, hearts, a note. A number on it says how much is new,
and from the other tabs the same number sits on Today. Tap a line to open the meal. The bell
works without notifications being switched on; it fills whenever the phones sync.

### A recipe from a link

Found something on Pinterest or a recipe site? Tap **+**, then **A recipe from a link**,
paste the link and tap **Read recipe**. Fork reads the page and fills in the dish form:
name, ingredients, steps, times, the picture and where it is from. Look it over, change
what is off and tap **Save dish**. Nothing is saved before that.

- **Three switches** decide how Fork writes it: in your language, with cups, ounces and
  Fahrenheit converted, and with the page's picture. The app remembers them.
- **Please check.** Where Fork was not sure (no number of servings, a rough conversion),
  a note on top of the form says so, with a jump to that part.
- **Even shorter on Android:** in Pinterest or the browser tap **Share** and pick
  **FoodwishUs**. The link arrives in the reader. This needs the installed app.
- **When a page cannot be read**, the app says why, and **Add it by hand** opens the empty
  form with the link kept as the source.

Fork gets help from an AI (Claude), which can misread things. That is why you always get
the form to check. Reading needs a connection, and a kitchen can have 40 pages read a day.

### The basket and the pantry

The **basket** is the shopping list the two of you share. It lives behind the basket
button on Today (next to the bell), and **+** has "Something for the basket".

- **Write it down:** type a thing and press Enter. "Milk 2 l" works, "2 l milk" too, and
  several at once with commas. The same thing is never there twice.
- **In the shop:** tap the circle and the thing is bought. The other phone sees it at once.
- **From a dish:** on a dish, **Ingredients into the basket** lists the ingredients to
  tick. What is in the pantry or on the basket already starts unticked.
- **Back home:** **Clear** empties what was bought, and can note it in the pantry in the
  same tap.

The **pantry** is what is at home, written the way you see it: "Flour 1 kg", "Milk, almost
empty", or just "Butter". Tap a line to change how much is left. **Used up** takes it out
and puts it on the basket. With **Tell Fork what is there** you write or dictate one
sentence ("we still have 2 onions, half a kilo of flour and the milk is almost gone") and
Fork sorts it into lines for you to look over.

**Fork checks the plan.** On the basket, **Check the plan** compares the meals planned for
the next 7 days with the pantry and suggests what to buy, each with a reason ("For the
Rüeblisuppe on Friday: the pantry says almost empty"). You decide: **+** puts a suggestion
on the list, **x** drops it. When you change the plan, Fork checks by himself and rings
the bell on both phones if something is missing; a switch on the basket turns that off.

With an empty pantry, without a connection, or before the AI helper is set up, Fork
compares names only: every ingredient of the planned meals that is not in the pantry and
not on the list. He says so when he does.

### When plans change

Nobody plans every meal a week ahead. These are the short ways:

- **Decided on the spot.** Tap **+**, then **A meal for today**. Pick a wish, anything from
  the recipe book, or type a name nobody has written down yet: it becomes a dish and today's
  meal in one tap. The Chef then finds **Served** on the meal, the Taster the proof photo.
- **Eating it sooner.** A meal that is planned for another day, or is only a wish, has
  **Serve it now** for the Chef and "Already on the table? Take the proof photo" for the
  Taster. It counts for the day it was really eaten.
- **The other one cooks after all.** Open the meal and tap **Change who cooks**. The hearts
  start over, because they belong to whoever eats. This works until a verdict has been started.
- **It never made it.** A meal that is already marked as served can still be skipped by
  either of you, with a reason if you like ("The oven went on strike").
- **Logged by mistake.** An eaten meal can be removed, with its photos and its verdict, for
  as long as the verdict can still be changed (24 hours). After the seal it is history.
- **Forgot to log it.** Take the proof photo from the gallery: the app reads when the photo
  was taken. Or correct the time under the photo.

## What gets rated

Under **Settings, Review criteria** you decide what gets its own row of spoons in a
review. Six are there from the start: Taste, Texture, Smell, Looks, Portion and
Temperature. You can add your own, rename them, give them an icon and drag them into
another order. Switch one off and it is no longer asked. Its old ratings stay in the
reviews and in the Cockpit.

## Backup: keep a copy, bring it back

Your kitchen lives on your phones and in the cloud, so losing one phone loses nothing.
A backup is the extra safety net, and it is yours to keep. Once a month is a good habit.

**Save a backup**

1. Open **Settings, Backup**.
2. Leave **Include photos** on for a full copy. Switch it off for a small file without
   pictures.
3. Tap **Save backup**. The file lands in the downloads of your phone. Its name looks like
   `foodwishus-backup-2026-11-10.zip`.
4. Put the file somewhere safe: your cloud drive, a computer, an email to yourself.

It is an ordinary zip file. Inside are your photos as normal pictures and one file with
everything else. Profiles, PINs and devices are never part of a backup.

**Bring a backup back**

1. Open **Settings, Backup** in the kitchen that should get the data.
2. Tap **Choose a backup file** and pick the file.
3. The app shows what is in it (how many dishes, meals and photos, and from when) and what
   will happen. Nothing has changed yet.
4. Tap **Import backup**.

Importing only ever adds. What is already in your kitchen stays as it is, unless the file
has a newer version. Things you deleted since the backup come back. Nothing is deleted,
and everything that comes in is shared with your partner like any other change. Importing
the same file twice does no harm.

If you ever start a brand-new kitchen, this is how the old one moves in. Invite your
partner first and import afterwards: then every dish and every review finds its owner.

## When something is off

**"Offline" or "changes waiting".** Carry on. Everything you do is saved on your phone and
sent by itself as soon as there is a connection again. The two phones do not have to be
online at the same time.

**Your partner does not see what you added.** Open **Settings, Sync details**. The page
says in plain words what is going on and what to do. **Sync now** tries right away.

**"Changes the cloud refused".** Rare, but it can happen. Each refused change is listed by
name on the same page, with two buttons. **Try again** sends it once more. **Discard**
gives up on it for good. Nothing is thrown away before you decide.

**It still does not work.** On the Sync page, tap **Copy details** and send the text to
whoever set the app up for you. It holds the app version, the state of the sync and error
codes. It holds no names, dishes or notes.

**Forgotten PIN.** The admin opens **Settings, People**, taps **Let ... back in** and
sends the one-time code. With it you choose a new PIN.

**Lost phone.** Log in on another device with your name and PIN, then open **Settings, PIN
and devices** and tap **Log out my other devices**. The admin can do the same for the
partner under **People**.

**"This device is logged out" or "Your seat is paused".** Somebody logged this device out,
or the admin paused the seat. Log in again with name and PIN, or ask the admin to switch
the seat on again. What you wrote in the meantime is still on the phone and is sent
afterwards.

## For the owner of the cloud

This part is for the person who set up the Supabase project that holds the data.

**The master key.** If the admin forgets their PIN and has no logged-in device left, nobody
in the app can help. You can: open the Supabase dashboard, go to **SQL Editor** and run

```sql
select private.rescue_code('Leo');   -- the name of the profile to get back into
```

It answers with a one-time code that is valid for 48 hours. On the app's start screen, tap
**I have a code**, type it and choose a new PIN. This command cannot be reached from the app.

**If the project went to sleep.** A free Supabase project pauses after about a week
without activity. Two automatic pings prevent that. If it pauses anyway, open the Supabase
dashboard and press **Restore project**. Nothing is lost.

**The three switches for the very first setup**

1. GitHub, make the repository public: Settings, General, Danger Zone, Change visibility.
   Only the code becomes visible. Meals and photos stay private in Supabase.
2. GitHub, turn on hosting: Settings, Pages, Build and deployment, Source: **GitHub Actions**.
3. Supabase, allow device logins: Authentication, Sign In / Providers, turn on **Allow
   anonymous sign-ins**. The app has no email or password. Each device gets its own
   invisible login, and people are recognised by their name and PIN.

Open the kitchen yourself right after the first deploy: whoever opens it is its admin.

**What it costs.** Nothing. GitHub Pages hosts the app and Supabase's free plan holds the
data: room for about 2,500 meal photos. If that ever gets tight, save a backup with photos
first.

### Switching notifications on (once)

Notifications need a small helper in the Supabase project. Nobody has to copy any key:
`supabase/functions/push/README.md` has the steps (one database update, one function, two
statements). Until that is done, everything else works and the Notifications page says it
is not set up yet.

### Switching Fork's helper on (once)

Reading recipes from links, checking the plan against the pantry and sorting a sentence
into pantry lines need a second small helper in the Supabase project and a key for the
Claude API. The key is the only thing in the whole app that costs money: well under a
cent per question. `supabase/functions/ai/README.md` has the steps (one database update,
one function, one secret that only you ever see). Until that is done, everything else
works: the reader says it is not set up yet, and Fork compares names.

### The basket and the pantry need one database update (once)

Apply `supabase/migrations/0009_basket.sql`. It adds two tables with the same rules as
the rest of the kitchen and changes nothing that exists. Until then the app keeps both
lists on each phone and shares them as soon as the update is there. Nothing is lost and
nothing is refused.

## For developers

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173, with a pretend cloud built into the dev server
npm run typecheck
npm test           # unit tests: sync, log-in and admin rules, backup, criteria, every Cockpit number
npm run e2e        # Playwright at Android size, against the dev server and its pretend cloud
npm run build      # production build (talks to Supabase)
npm run build:ai   # the recipe helper as one file for deploying (build:push does the same for notifications)
npm run icons      # regenerate app icons from the mascot
```

Development never touches real data: `npm run dev` and all tests use a pretend cloud
(`src/data/fake`) that follows the same rules as the database in `supabase/migrations`.
Open `/#/lab` in two browser windows to play with sync by hand, and `/#/styleguide` to see
every component. [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) has the milestones, [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) the
reasons, [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) the rules of the house.

| Folder | What lives there |
| --- | --- |
| `src/data` | Types, local database, outbox, sync engine, actions, backup, Supabase adapter |
| `src/ui` | Design tokens, components, the deco kit and Fork the mascot |
| `src/features` | Screens, one folder per part of the app |
| `src/i18n`, `src/content` | English and German copy, quotes |
| `src/lib` | Dates (the kitchen lives in Europe/Zurich) and the Cockpit's numbers |
| `supabase/migrations` | Database schema and security rules, in order |
| `supabase/tests` | Security test to run in the Supabase SQL editor |
| `tests/unit`, `tests/e2e` | Unit tests and browser tests |
| `brand` | Logo files |
